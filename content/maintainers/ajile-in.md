- Test the platform and share feedback on real-world use cases
- Spread the word about open standards for decentralized identity

## A small brief about your project

CREDEBL is an LFDT project — an open-source, multi-tenant, agent-agnostic and ledger-agnostic platform for issuing, holding and verifying verifiable credentials at population scale. Recognized as a Digital Public Good by the DPGA, it serves as the protocol layer for Bhutan's National Digital Identity (NDI) and powered the Digi Yatra Foundation's international digital travel credentials PoC during the IATA Contactless trials with IndiGo and BLR Airport.

## One FOSS maintainer lesson for your younger self

Build in the open from day one. Standards, security and community are the things you cannot retrofit later — the earlier the ecosystem's eyes are on your architecture, the less painful the scaling gets.

## Why do you do it? Why do you bother maintaining a FOSS project?

Decentralized identity is a public good — it gives people back control of their own data. Keeping CREDEBL open source accelerates the adoption of self-sovereign identity worldwide, and knowing the platform issues credentials for millions of citizens is what makes the late-night issue triage worth it.

## Which file in your project would you most like to set on fire?

The ever-growing pile of docker-compose*.yml flavours in the platform repo — dev, nats, openbao, redis, prod. Each one is a reminder that easy local setup is a lie, but they keep the onboarding story alive, so I keep them.

## What's your open-source villain origin story?

I watched closed, walled-garden identity systems treat people's data as database rows they happened to rent. After too many reimplementations of the same credential flows, I became the guy who pushes open standards, multi-tenancy and ledger-agnostic design to everyone who will listen.
